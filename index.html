How this works. Fernrow’s ordering runs on Toast and its bookings run on Resy — the tools the bakehouse already pays for and its staff already know. This website doesn’t try to replace them. It sends customers straight into them, so tickets and covers land where the team is already looking. On a live build, these two buttons point directly at the business’s own Toast and Resy pages.

The bakehouse

A morning, in order

Everything here is made in the room you walk into. The oven is behind the counter, not in a basement across town.

Since 2016

A folding table, then a lease

Fernrow started as a Saturday bread table outside a friend’s flower shop. Six batches, one folding table, sold out by nine.

Two years later we took the lease on Division and put the oven where everyone could see it. The method hasn’t changed much: slow ferments, flour milled in the Willamette Valley, and a menu short enough that we can do all of it properly.

Mornings are pastry and espresso. Lunch is whatever the kitchen is excited about that week. Wednesday through Sunday there’s bread by the loaf until it’s gone — and it does go.

No table service and no reservations for two. But the long table at the back is yours if you book it.

Concept project. Fernrow Bakehouse & Coffee is a fictional business invented to demonstrate what a one-page website for an independent café looks like. The name, address, phone number, email, menu and prices are made up. There is no real shop at this address, no order will reach a kitchen, and no table can be booked.
